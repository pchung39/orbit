## 4. Isolating the Suspect Load
- The heater draw (3.73 A) is far outside its healthy post-enable range and independently exceeds its own critical limit — a clear off-nominal load by the ≥2× criterion in EPS-17. [DERIVED]
- The payload current, despite the coincidental `SCIENCE_MODE` entry 3 seconds before the warn, is sitting at its expected SCIENCE-mode level (~0.9 A) and has not crossed its own warn limit. Payload current alone (<1 A) cannot account for a multi-amp bus disturbance. [DERIVED]
- The bus-current warn crossing (6.30 A vs 6.0 A) is consistent with the combined heater overdraw plus the payload's normal SCIENCE-mode draw, not with the payload alone. [DERIVED]
- Per EPS-17 step 5: the `SCIENCE_MODE` change is a confounder, not proof — it is not being used to close the investigation, and heater current was explicitly checked before considering the payload. [DOCUMENTED — this procedure]

## 5. Similar Incident Search
- `INC-0187` ("Heater B overcurrent during eclipse") was retrieved as a directly matching prior case. [DOCUMENTED]
- INC-0187 root cause: a partial short in Heater B causing ~3.2× normal current draw on `HEATER_B_ENABLE`, sagging the bus into a voltage warn with payload idle (no confounder). Closed as `HEATER_B_OVERCURRENT` (FAULT-001). [DOCUMENTED]
- INC-0187 explicitly notes it is the control case for EPS-204: same heater signature, but EPS-204 additionally has a coincidental `SCIENCE_MODE` entry that also nudges bus current over its warn line, which could mislead an investigator into blaming the payload. [DOCUMENTED]
- The EPS-204 heater current (3.73 A) closely matches the INC-0187 heater signature (~3.7 A). [DERIVED]

## 6. Hypothesis
Heater B has drawn ~3.1–3.2× its normal current immediately following `HEATER_B_ENABLE` at 14:29:44, sagging the bus below the 26.5 V warn threshold by 14:31:55. This matches the closed fault `HEATER_B_OVERCURRENT` (FAULT-001) documented in `INC-0187`. The near-simultaneous `SCIENCE_MODE` entry is a confounder: payload current remains within its normal SCIENCE-mode band and cannot explain the voltage sag on its own; it only marginally pushes bus current over its own separate warn limit. [HYPOTHESIS]
