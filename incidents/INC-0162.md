## What happened (say this in an interview)

Eclipse. The operator enabled Heater B. Heater current was the usual **~1.2 A**. Battery terminal voltage dropped through **25.5 V** anyway. Payload stayed STANDBY. The pack's internal resistance had grown — ordinary discharge was enough. Same fault family as the BATT-003 tape. Do not inhibit a healthy heater.

## What was ruled out

- **Heater overcurrent** — 1.17 A is the healthy ON family, not INC-0187's ~3.7 A. **[OBSERVED]**
- **Payload** — STANDBY, ~0.05 A. **[OBSERVED]**
- **Empty pack** — state of charge was still high; the sag is IR under discharge, not SOC. **[DERIVED]**

## Root cause (close-out)

Pack internal resistance degraded. Terminal voltage sags under ordinary eclipse discharge. **[DOCUMENTED]** at close-out; this was a **[HYPOTHESIS]** until load currents were shown healthy.

Matches fault library `BATTERY_RESISTANCE_DEGRADATION` (FAULT-003).

## Why a later battery-voltage warn should retrieve this

EPS-204 is a heater at 3×. This incident is the control in the other direction: **heater healthy, pack sags, eclipse**. If a battery-voltage investigation surfaces INC-0162, do not walk EPS-17 and inhibit Heater B.
