## What happened (say this in an interview)

During eclipse the operator enabled Heater B. Heater current went to **~3.7 A** instead of the usual **~1.2 A**. The bus sagged and nipped the 26.5 V warn. The payload stayed in STANDBY the whole time, so there was nothing else to blame. Root cause: a partial short in Heater B. Same fault family as the EPS-204 demo, without the coincidental science-mode entry.

## What was ruled out

- **Payload** — `PAY.mode` remained STANDBY; payload current stayed ~0.08 A. A science-mode turn-on was not in the command log. **[OBSERVED]**
- **Solar / eclipse** — the sag started at heater enable, not at eclipse entry (eclipse had already been in progress). **[OBSERVED]**
- **Battery empty** — state of charge was ~95% at enable. **[DERIVED]**

## Root cause (close-out)

Partial short in Heater B's resistive element: the heater draws ~3.2× current for the same ON command. **[DOCUMENTED]** at close-out; this was a **[HYPOTHESIS]** until current was compared to the previous healthy `HEATER_B_ENABLE`.

Matches fault library `HEATER_B_OVERCURRENT` (FAULT-001).

## Why EPS-204 should retrieve this

EPS-204 is the same heater fault with an extra SCIENCE_MODE entry two minutes later. That extra step trips bus-current warn and makes the payload look guilty. This incident is the control case: **heater 3×, payload idle, bus voltage warn, no bus-current warn.** If an investigation of EPS-204 surfaces INC-0187, the operator can see that the heater signature alone is enough.
