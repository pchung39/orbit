ORBIT · Spacecraft anomaly investigation

From spacecraft alarm to a decision you can explain.

ORBIT brings telemetry, command history, procedures, and prior incidents into one reviewable case. It helps operators assess what happened, examine the evidence, and decide what to do next—including when to hold for more information.

Designed and built by Paul Chung

Product manager and software engineer with experience building spacecraft ground software and test automation at NASA JPL.

Similar alarms. Different next steps.

Both cases trigger a low-voltage warning. Both include a payload entering science mode. The deciding evidence is whether heater current meets the procedure’s threshold for recommending an inhibit.

How it works

An investigation you can follow.

  1. 01

    Preserve the evidence

    Save a fixed window of telemetry and events so the investigation can be reviewed against the same inputs later.

  2. 02

    Assess it in context

    Use procedures and prior incidents to interpret the measurements and assess whether they support a recommendation.

  3. 03

    Show the basis for the decision

    Present the evidence, calculations, citations, and proposed explanation together. Hold when the available evidence is insufficient.

Where the AI fits

In this workflow, the investigator decides what evidence to examine, uses tools to retrieve or calculate it, and assesses whether the results support a conclusion.

ORBIT’s browser demo uses a rules-based investigator to establish repeatable reference behavior. Experimental LLM providers are available separately through the command line. The published evaluation results cover the rules-based provider.

Evaluation

A correct answer is only part of the test.

The suite checks both the decision and the evidence supporting it. A report can reach the expected conclusion and still fail because its claims are unsupported or its recommendation is inappropriate.
